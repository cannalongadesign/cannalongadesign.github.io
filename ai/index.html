How I work with AI4 min read

AI on both sides of the screen

I use AI the way I design: it proposes, a person decides, and every claim has evidence behind it. Here is how that played out, product by product: what the AI does, where a person stays in charge, and what changed.

1

Safe Site Detector

Consumer trust
What the AI does

A crawler collects public signals about a store and the company behind it, and a model writes the analysis. Engineering built it AI-first, so the first version was live in about six weeks.

Where a person decides

The model can gather far more than a shopper needs, and a wrong claim about a company can harm a legitimate business. I turned its output into one verdict, from 0 to 5 shields, worded to state only what was verified, with each shield showing the signal behind it.

What changed

45.1% → 72.4% said the detector helped them decide. Monthly cost went from a projected R$1.7M to R$1.2k. Citations in Google AI Overview rose 138% across 30 companies.

2

LIAM

Clinical AI
What the AI does

The therapist narrates the session as it happens, and LIAM turns the audio into a first draft of every required field of the clinical record, rewritten into sentences that read like a record, not a transcript.

Where a person decides

LIAM proposes and never submits. Every field is a suggestion the therapist copies in by hand, the recording stays attached to check any line, and a prompt in the flow asks whether the suggestions are still good.

What changed

Documentation went from up to 2 hours to about 25 minutes per session, as reported by the clinical team a quarter after rollout. The clinical lead’s verdict was that record quality improved.

3

qa-design

AI challenge championMy design process
What the AI does

A Claude skill that reviews each build against the Figma file, writes a fix file the developer runs in their own Claude or Cursor, and opens the Jira task.

Where a person decides

When something can’t be resolved from the design alone, the skill asks me instead of guessing. The developer reviews every fix before applying it, and I take a last look before production.

What changed

The review every experiment needed stayed in place, without the queue. The first real run found 30+ inconsistencies, all fixed in one go except one design decision. One of 4 champions out of 353 prototypes in the company’s AI challenge.

4

This site

For AI readers
What the AI does

I built it with Claude Code: plain HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript, no framework. People increasingly ask an AI assistant before they open a website, so it is written for that second reader too.

Where a person decides

Only numbers I can back up: every result says how it was measured, and anything I could not verify is left out. An AI repeats what it finds, so the page has to be right first.

What changed

Structured data, an llms.txt and crawlable text on every page, so an assistant asked about my work quotes it correctly and points back here.
